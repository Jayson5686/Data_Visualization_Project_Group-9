DATA Sources, measures & interpretation

About the data

A brief guide to what these visualisations include and how to interpret the figures.

01

Source

Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), National Hospital Morbidity Database (NHMD). The dashboard uses the project’s prepared annual datasets.

02

Coverage

Australian road-crash hospitalisation records from 2011 to 2021, shown nationally and across states and territories, road-user groups, age groups, and First Nations views.

03

Measures

Hospitalisations count admissions; bed days represent inpatient days associated with those admissions. Some summary figures note the exclusion of deaths within 30 days.

How to read the figures

These are recorded counts, not population-adjusted rates. Differences in totals between jurisdictions may reflect population size as well as underlying patterns, and the records do not represent every road crash or injury.

Suppressed values: “n.p.” means a value is not published to protect privacy. It is not zero; hatched marks identify these values in the First Nations charts.

Questions & answers

Understanding the data

What do these measures mean?

Hospitalisations are the number of hospital admissions in the dataset. Bed days count inpatient days associated with those admissions. They describe different things: a longer stay can contribute more bed days without adding another admission.

Can every year be compared directly?

Use year-to-year comparisons as a guide, not as proof that every change reflects a change in road safety. The dashboard marks known comparability breaks and notable context, including changes in reporting and the 2020 pandemic period. Check these annotations when interpreting a trend.

Why do the chart totals differ?

Charts show different measures, years, and breakdowns. A national total, a selected state, and a road-user or age-group category do not have the same scope. Some First Nations breakdown values are suppressed as n.p., so a visible subtotal may not equal a chart’s overall total.

What can counts tell us about risk?

Counts show how many admissions were recorded; they are not population-adjusted rates. A larger count does not by itself mean an individual in that group had a higher risk. Population size, travel exposure, and other context would be needed to compare risk fairly.
